About

I work as an Application Technical Engineer at IdeaScale, where I spend my days helping users get the most out of complex software. Outside of work, I think a lot about how people actually experience the technology around them: what frustrates them, what feels natural, and what gets quietly ignored because it's just too hard to use.

That curiosity is what led me to HCI research. My focus is on the everyday technology experiences of people in Bangladesh, particularly communities whose needs rarely shape the design of the tools they end up using. I believe the gap between how a system is designed and how it is actually lived with is where the most interesting and most important — design problems hide.

During my undergraduate years at United International University, I worked on projects that tried to solve real problems close to home — from giving online shoppers control over who holds their personal data, to helping farmers access agricultural supplies without the burden of upfront costs. Both projects were recognized at UIU's Project Showcase in Software Engineering ('22) and System Design ('22).

I hold a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science from United International University. Throughout my academic and professional journey, I have been known as Rabius Sani Jabiullah in all formal contexts, including publications.

Currently researching: Dark patterns in Bangladeshi mobile apps, how manipulative design choices affect everyday users, and what a more honest design practice could look like in this context.

Recent highlight: Dec '25 — “Between Control and Safety: Purpose-driven Location Sharing Experiences in Bangladesh” accepted as first-author at ICCIT 2025.

Shohoj Thikana [github]

In online shopping, customers must share personal details like home addresses, phone numbers, and house information with shop owners and delivery organizations. This poses security risks as it becomes challenging to monitor or trace the usage of this sensitive information. Once shared, customers lose control over their data, leading to potential security breaches.

Shohoj Thikana allows users to monitor and modify their sensitive information quickly. Users can see who has accessed their data, update their addresses, and add relevant details like images or voice directions. They can also customize and shorten their location addresses for convenience.

This project significantly enhances user security and control over personal information. It allows users to manage their data proactively, ensuring privacy and customization. It was recognized as the runner-up in the Software Engineering Lab at the CSE Project Show ('22).

Krishok Bondhu [github]

Bangladesh's agricultural sector faces severe challenges due to high loan interest rates, lengthy paperwork, and inadequate risk management. These issues make it difficult for farmers to secure the necessary funds for cultivation, leading many to abandon farming, which threatens the country's economy.

The proposed solution, Krishok Bondhu, introduces a Grow-Now-Pay-Later (GNPL) policy. This system allows farmers to acquire essential agricultural products like seeds and fertilizer without immediate full payment. Instead, they can repay the costs after harvest, reducing financial stress and encouraging continuous cultivation.

This project won first place in the System Design Project Showcase at United International University ('22).

Implementing the Krishok Bondhu system has the potential to revolutionize agriculture in Bangladesh by providing farmers with the financial flexibility to continue their work without the burden of high-interest loans. This approach stabilizes farmers' incomes and promotes sustainable agricultural practices, significantly enhancing the livelihoods of millions of farmers in rural areas.

Research

Prioritizing the essential concerns of users lies at the core of my research. Between Control and Safety: Purpose-driven Location Sharing Experiences in Bangladesh

Rabius Sani Jabiullah, Khandakar Nur Md. Shakil, Prianka Akter
27th International Conference on Computer and Information Technology (ICCIT 2025)
Accepted — December 2025

This study examines purpose-driven location sharing among smartphone users in Bangladesh, focusing on how individuals navigate control, trust, and safety across everyday contexts such as deliveries, transportation, and social coordination. Findings reveal that users frequently rely on informal workarounds including typed addresses, landmark descriptions, images, and voice notes due to usability and control limitations in existing platforms. The research further identifies how disclosed location data becomes difficult to retract, raising concerns around misuse, screenshots, and long-term retention. Gendered experiences are also examined, with women and gender-diverse users found to adopt heightened precautionary strategies while continuing to face elevated safety risks. The study surfaces key socio-technical gaps in current location-sharing systems and proposes design opportunities for more context-aware, trust-sensitive, and gender-inclusive HCI solutions.
